## 1. Research Problem and Operational Motivation

Payment-card fraud detection systems (FDS) operate under two strict real-world constraints that standard static graph learning benchmarks routinely violate:
1. **Strict Causal Temporal Visibility:** When scoring an incoming transaction $i$ at authorization or review timestamp $t_i$, the detector can only observe transactions $j$ with $t_j \le t_i$ and entity states constructed exclusively from events up to $t_i$. Static multi-relational graphs that connect all transactions across an entire dataset to shared entity hubs (such as card identifiers, billing addresses, email domains, or device fingerprints) create two-hop message-passing pathways through which future transaction attributes and future topological density leak backward into historical predictions ([Khaleghpour & McKinney, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06632); [Maganti, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19514)).
2. **Verification Latency and Label Maturity:** As established by [Dal Pozzolo et al. (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1109/IJCNN.2015.7280527) and [Dal Pozzolo et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1109/TNNLS.2017.2736643), the vast majority of payment-card transactions are not inspected by human analysts at the time of authorization. Instead, ground-truth fraud labels arrive asynchronously after a stochastic reporting or chargeback delay $D_i > 0$ (often spanning days to weeks, as documented in the compromised-card blocking study of [Nguyen et al., 2022](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v151/bach-nguyen22a.html)). At any model retraining or feature-aggregation cutoff $	au$, recent fraudulent transactions whose report time $t_i + D_i > 	au$ appear identical to legitimate transactions. Treating these unreported, immature transactions as confirmed negatives (Rule $V_0$) injects systematic one-sided label noise into both the training loss and historical neighbor-risk aggregations.

Furthermore, recent benchmark evidence from [Tang et al. (2023, GADBench)](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/5eaafd67434a4cfb1cf829722c65f184-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.html) demonstrates that gradient-boosted decision trees augmented with explicit neighborhood feature aggregations frequently match or outperform specialized Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) on static anomaly detection benchmarks. Consequently, architectural complexity alone cannot establish a scientific contribution unless evaluated against strong causal neighbor-aggregate tabular baselines under realistic label-maturity constraints.

## 3. Research Questions and Falsifiable Hypotheses

### 3.1 Primary and Secondary Research Questions
* **RQ1 (Topology vs. Label Leakage Attribution):** How much of a graph model's apparent advantage over a strong tabular or tree-with-neighbor-features baseline remains when evaluated under strict causal temporal visibility ($P_3$) compared with transductive or oracle visibility ($P_0, P_1$)?
* **RQ2 (Label-Maturity-Aware Supervision):** Under stochastic verification latency ($D_1, D_2, D_3$), does maturity-aware Bayesian soft-target supervision ($V_2$) and expected hidden-fraud neighbor aggregation improve operational ranking (Average Precision) and alert-budget precision ($	ext{Precision}@1\%$) compared with naive unreported-as-negative training ($V_0$) and immature-row dropping ($V_1$)?
* **RQ3 (Locus of Relational Utility):** Under what topological conditions (specifically visible prior fraud-neighbor coverage within two hops, relation type, and hub degree capping) does relational structure add predictive value beyond raw tabular features?
* **RQ4 (Robustness & Misspecification Sensitivity):** How sensitive are maturity-aware learning and relational aggregation to delay-distribution misspecification, fraud-prior estimation error, maturity-horizon truncation, and hub concentration?

### 3.2 Pre-Registered Testable Hypotheses and Interpretation Rules
* **Hypothesis H1 (Leakage Shrinkage):** Let $G_{	ext{full}} = 	ext{AP}(	ext{GNN}, P_0/P_1) - 	ext{AP}(	ext{Tree+Neighbor}, P_0/P_1)$ under future/transductive visibility and $G_{	ext{causal}} = 	ext{AP}(	ext{GNN}, P_3) - 	ext{AP}(	ext{Tree+Neighbor}, P_3)$ under causal visibility. We hypothesize that $G_{	ext{full}} > G_{	ext{causal}}$ and that removing future topology and label leakage substantially shrinks or eliminates the apparent GNN advantage over causal neighbor-aggregate trees. *Interpretation Rule:* If $G_{	ext{full}} \le 0$, report directly that GNNs did not outperform tree+neighbor baselines even under unrestricted visibility, and report absolute paired differences with $95\%$ block-bootstrap confidence intervals rather than a ratio.
* **Hypothesis H2 (Maturity-Aware Supervision $V_2$ vs. $V_0/V_1$):** Across rolling evaluation blocks under delayed feedback ($D_1, D_2, D_3$), training and aggregating with Bayesian posterior soft targets ($V_2$) improves $	ext{Precision}@1\%$ and $	ext{AP}$ over naive $V_0$ (treating unreported rows as negative) and matches or outperforms $V_1$ (dropping immature rows from the loss while retaining causal context), while yielding superior probability calibration (lower Brier score). *Interpretation Rule:* $V_2$ must beat both $V_0$ and $V_1$ on paired evaluation blocks to claim a headline detection gain; if $V_2$ significantly beats $V_0$ in ranking and calibration but is statistically indistinguishable from $V_1$ in top-$1\%$ ranking, position $V_2$ as a well-calibrated probabilistic formulation and $V_1$ as a competitive operational alternative.
* **Hypothesis H3 (Visible Fraud-Neighbor Stratum Effect):** Relational models ($B_1, B_2, B_3, B_5$) achieve their primary performance gains over raw tabular baselines ($B_0$) on the stratum of evaluation transactions that possess at least one **causally prior, visibly reported** fraudulent neighbor within two hops at the scoring timestamp, whereas performance on isolated transactions matches raw tabular models. *Interpretation Rule:* Partition test transactions strictly by *decision-time visible* prior fraud neighbors (never by final oracle labels) and report stratum sample sizes, prevalence, and paired metric deltas.
* **Hypothesis H4 (Hub Control & Stability):** Bounding extreme shared-entity hubs via degree capping ($K \in \{10, 50, 200\}$) and inverse-log degree weighting $w(e) = 1/\ln(2 + 	ext{deg}_{\le t}(e))$ reduces block-to-block AP dispersion and improves average detection over uncapped multi-relational graphs where high-degree public email domains or billing addresses dilute fraud signals.

## 4. Mathematical Formulation of Causal Graph Construction and Label Maturity

### 4.1 Event-Time Causal Graph Representation
Let $\mathcal{T} = \{x_i, e_i, t_i, Y_i\}_{i=1}^N$ denote a chronologically ordered sequence of payment transactions, where $x_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is the vector of authorization-available transaction attributes, $e_i = (e_{i,1}, \dots, e_{i,R})$ is the tuple of $R$ relational entity proxies (e.g., card proxy, address proxy, email domain, merchant/terminal), $t_i \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ is the transaction timestamp, and $Y_i \in \{0, 1\}$ is the true underlying fraud indicator ($Y_i=1$ for fraud, $Y_i=0$ for legitimate).

Under verification latency, each fraudulent transaction ($Y_i = 1$) experiences a stochastic reporting delay $D_i \ge 0$ drawn from a delay distribution with cumulative distribution function $F_D(d) = \mathbb{P}(D_i \le d \mid Y_i = 1)$ and survival function $S(a) = 1 - F_D(a) = \mathbb{P}(D_i > a \mid Y_i = 1)$, with hard maturity horizon $M$ such that $S(a) = 0$ for all $a \ge M$. The fraud report arrival timestamp is $t_i^{	ext{rep}} = t_i + D_i$ if $Y_i = 1$, and $t_i^{	ext{rep}} = +\infty$ if $Y_i = 0$ (assuming zero false-positive dispute reports in the baseline generative process).

At any historical retraining or scoring cutoff $	au$, a historical transaction $i$ ($t_i \le 	au$) has age $a_i(	au) = 	au - t_i \ge 0$ and belongs to exactly one of three observable label states $Z_i(	au) \in \{1, 0, 	ext{imm}\}$:
1. **Visible Positive ($Z_i(	au) = 1$):** $t_i^{	ext{rep}} \le 	au$ (a fraud report has arrived by $	au$).
2. **Mature Negative ($Z_i(	au) = 0$):** $t_i^{	ext{rep}} > 	au$ and $a_i(	au) \ge M$ (no fraud report has arrived and the transaction age equals or exceeds the scenario's maximum delay horizon $M$).
3. **Immature / Unreported ($Z_i(	au) = 	ext{imm}$):** $t_i^{	ext{rep}} > 	au$ and $0 \le a_i(	au) < M$ (no fraud report has arrived, but the transaction is younger than $M$ and could still be an unreported fraud).

Any future transaction with $t_i > 	au$ is completely excluded from the observable state $\mathcal{F}_	au$.

### 4.2 Bayesian Posterior Derivation for Immature Transactions ($V_2$)
For an immature, unreported transaction ($Z_i(	au) = 	ext{imm}$) of age $a_i = 	au - t_i \in [0, M)$, let $\pi_	au = \mathbb{P}(Y = 1)$ denote the time-safe prior fraud prevalence estimated exclusively from mature historical cohorts at cutoff $	au$. Under the assumptions that (i) legitimate transactions never generate fraud reports ($\mathbb{P}(t_i^{	ext{rep}} > 	au \mid Y_i = 0, a_i) = 1$) and (ii) fraudulent transactions remain unreported at age $a_i$ with survival probability $S(a_i) = \mathbb{P}(D_i > a_i \mid Y_i = 1)$, Bayes' theorem yields the exact posterior probability that the unreported transaction is fraudulent:
$$q(a_i; \pi_	au) = \mathbb{P}(Y_i = 1 \mid Z_i(	au) = 	ext{imm}, a_i) = rac{\pi_	au S(a_i)}{\pi_	au S(a_i) + (1 - \pi_	au)}$$

**Boundary Properties:**
* At age $a_i = 0$ (assuming $S(0) = 1$), $q(0; \pi_	au) = \pi_	au$.
* Since $S(a)$ is monotonically non-increasing on $[0, M]$, $q(a_i; \pi_	au)$ decreases monotonically with transaction age $a_i$ as the absence of a fraud report provides increasing evidence that the transaction is legitimate.
* At the maturity boundary $a_i \ge M$, $q(a_i; \pi_	au) = 0$ if and only if $S(M) = 0$ (which holds for bounded scenarios $D_1, D_2, D_3$ at their respective horizons $M \in \{14, 30, 90\}$ days).

### 4.3 Supervision Rules ($V_0, V_1, V_2, V_3$) and Expected Cross-Entropy Loss
Given model predicted probability $\hat{p}_i = \sigma(f_	heta(i, \mathcal{G}_{\le t_i})) \in [\epsilon, 1-\epsilon]$ (clipped at $\epsilon = 10^{-7}$), the binary cross-entropy for target $y \in [0, 1]$ is $\ell(y, \hat{p}_i) = -[y \ln \hat{p}_i + (1 - y)\ln(1 - \hat{p}_i)]$. At training cutoff $	au$, over historical training transactions $\mathcal{I}_{\le 	au} = \{i : t_i \le 	au\}$, the four supervision regimes are defined as:
* **$V_0$ (Naive Unreported-as-Negative):** Assigns target $	ilde{y}_i^{V_0} = 1$ if $Z_i(	au)=1$, and $	ilde{y}_i^{V_0} = 0$ if $Z_i(	au) \in \{0, 	ext{imm}\}$. Minimizes $\mathcal{L}_{V_0} = rac{1}{|\mathcal{I}_{\le 	au}|}\sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_{\le 	au}} \ell(	ilde{y}_i^{V_0}, \hat{p}_i)$.
* **$V_1$ (Drop-Immature Supervision):** Retains immature transactions in the causal graph for structural context, but masks them from the training loss: $\mathcal{I}_{	ext{mat}}(	au) = \{i \in \mathcal{I}_{\le 	au} : Z_i(	au) \in \{0, 1\}\}$. Minimizes $\mathcal{L}_{V_1} = rac{1}{|\mathcal{I}_{	ext{mat}}(	au)|}\sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_{	ext{mat}}(	au)} \ell(Z_i(	au), \hat{p}_i)$.
* **$V_2$ (Maturity-Aware Soft-Target Posterior):** Assigns $	ilde{y}_i^{V_2} = 1$ if $Z_i(	au)=1$, $	ilde{y}_i^{V_2} = 0$ if $Z_i(	au)=0$, and the Bayesian posterior $	ilde{y}_i^{V_2} = q(a_i(	au); \hat{\pi}_	au)$ if $Z_i(	au)=	ext{imm}$. Minimizes the expected binary cross-entropy $\mathcal{L}_{V_2} = rac{1}{|\mathcal{I}_{\le 	au}|}\sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}_{\le 	au}} \ell(	ilde{y}_i^{V_2}, \hat{p}_i)$.
* **$V_3$ (Non-Deployable Oracle Upper Bound):** Uses the true hidden label $Y_i \in \{0, 1\}$ for all $i \in \mathcal{I}_{\le 	au}$ regardless of reporting delay ($D_i = 0$). Reported strictly in separate oracle columns/panels as a non-deployable diagnostic.

### 4.4 Maturity-Aware Causal Neighbor Features and Message Passing
For any transaction $i$ scored at reference cutoff $	au_{	ext{ref}} = \max(	au_{	ext{train}}, t_i)$ and for each relation $r \in \{1, \dots, R\}$, let $\mathcal{N}_r^{<}(i) = \{j : t_j < t_i 	ext{ and } e_{j,r} = e_{i,r}\}$ denote the causally prior transactions sharing entity proxy $e_{i,r}$, capped to the $K$ most recent transactions within recency window $W$. Instead of leaking final labels $Y_j$ or conflating immature transactions with confirmed negatives, our maturity-aware neighbor aggregator computes four distinct relational signals per relation $r$:
1. **Visible Prior Fraud Count & Rate:** $c_{i,r}^{	ext{vis}} = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_r^{<}(i)} \mathbb{I}(Z_j(	au_{	ext{ref}}) = 1)$.
2. **Mature Negative Prior Count:** $c_{i,r}^{	ext{mat0}} = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_r^{<}(i)} \mathbb{I}(Z_j(	au_{	ext{ref}}) = 0)$.
3. **Immature Unreported Prior Count:** $c_{i,r}^{	ext{imm}} = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_r^{<}(i)} \mathbb{I}(Z_j(	au_{	ext{ref}}) = 	ext{imm})$.
4. **Expected Hidden Fraud Mass:** $m_{i,r}^{	ext{hid}} = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_r^{<}(i) : Z_j(	au_{	ext{ref}})=	ext{imm}} q(t_i - t_j; \hat{\pi}_	au)$, which estimates the expected number of not-yet-reported fraudulent transactions in the entity's recent history, combined with inverse-log hub weighting $w_{j,r} = 1/\ln(2 + |\mathcal{N}_r^{<}(j)|)$.

## 5. Evaluation Metrics and Statistical Analysis Plan
* **Primary Ranking Metric:** Average Precision ($	ext{AP}$), computed as the weighted sum of precisions achieved at each threshold with the increase in recall from the previous threshold as weight: $	ext{AP} = \sum_n (R_n - R_{n-1}) P_n$ ([Scikit-learn Documentation, R9](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/model_evaluation.html#precision-recall-and-f-measures)). Distinguished explicitly from trapezoidal PR-AUC.
* **Primary Operational Metric:** Alert-budget precision $	ext{Precision}@B\%$ for $B \in \{0.5\%, 1.0\%, 5.0\%\}$ (with $B=1.0\%$ primary) and corresponding $	ext{Recall}@B\%$, where the alert budget per evaluation block of size $N_b$ is $k_b = \lceil (B/100) \cdot N_b ceil$, with ties broken deterministically by higher transaction amount then earlier index.
* **Secondary Operational & Calibration Metrics:** Card-proxy Precision@$k$ (aggregated by maximum score per unique card proxy per block), Recall at $1\%$ validation-tuned FPR, and Brier score $	ext{BS} = rac{1}{N_b}\sum_{i=1}^{N_b} (\hat{p}_i - Y_i)^2$.
* **Statistical Inference:** Per-block paired differences $\Delta_b = M_{A,b} - M_{B,b}$ across $B_{	ext{test}}$ rolling temporal test blocks; $95\%$ moving-block bootstrap confidence intervals ($10,000$ resamples preserving temporal dependence); two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test ([SciPy Documentation, R10](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.wilcoxon.html)) with Holm-Bonferroni step-down multiplicity correction across the pre-registered comparison family.
